ONCOLOGY DIGITAL CARE PLATFORM
Master Functional, Data Governance, AI Governance & Solution Requirements
Cancer Care Hospital | EMR + Clinical Intelligence + MDT + Governance
Prepared from the supplied workflows and requirements | 22 August 2026
Executive positionThis is not only an EMR implementation. It is an integrated oncology care platform with four layers:1) transactional EMR/HMIS, 2) clinical data/interoperability, 3) AI/ML clinical intelligence, and4) Data Governance + AI Governance. Governance must be designed into every workflow from day one.
1. Executive Summary & Design Principles
The supplied requirements cover the patient journey from registration through intake, consultation, investigations, diagnosis, staging, treatment planning, MDT/tumor-board review, treatment, financial counselling and follow-up. The AI requirements add document intelligence, voice/conversation processing, clinical summarization, structured extraction, clinical decision support and oncology intelligence.
The EMR is the system of record; AI is an intelligence/decision-support layer.
Patient identity is mastered through an MPI. AI may detect possible duplicates but must not autonomously merge records.
Every AI-derived clinical fact must retain source, timestamp, model/version, status and human verification.
High-risk clinical AI must be human-in-the-loop.
Use deterministic rules for deterministic calculations and hard safety constraints; use LLMs for language-heavy tasks.
Interoperability, consent, privacy, audit and provenance are architectural requirements, not later enhancements.
Every AI capability needs an intended use, risk classification, validation criteria, owner, monitoring plan and rollback path.
Clinical safety is more important than automation percentage.

3.1 AI Gateway requirements
All production AI requests pass through a controlled gateway.
Authorize the user/application and enforce purpose-based access.
Minimize/mask unnecessary PII/PHI before model calls where appropriate.
Route only to approved models and versions.
Validate structured outputs and attach provenance.
Log AI usage and support model kill-switch/rollback.
Never allow application teams to call an unregistered production model directly.
4. Functional Requirements
4.1 Registration, MPI & Patient Identity
Create a unique hospital patient identity and Master Patient Index.
Support ABHA/ABDM linkage where applicable and consented.
Capture demographics, contact, photo and three-level referral-doctor hierarchy.
Detect duplicates using deterministic + probabilistic matching.
Potential duplicates go to an authorized reviewer; no autonomous merge.
Maintain merge/unmerge history and audit.
Digital consent must capture purpose, version, timestamp, status and withdrawal.
Scheduling, check-in, queue and location transitions must be linked to the encounter.
4.2 Nurse EMR / Intake
Upload/classify previous documents.
Capture vitals; calculate BMI and BSA deterministically.
Pain, fall-risk, ECOG and Karnofsky scoring.
Medication reconciliation and allergies/adverse reactions.
Structured oncology history: family, hormonal, reproductive and social history.
Patient longitudinal journey view.
Dynamic branching forms with validation, required fields and versioning.
Form Builder must have draft, review, publish, retire and audit states.
4.3 Doctor EMR / OPD
Physical examination and structured clinical documentation.
Schematic/body annotation and clinical image upload.
DICOM/PACS viewer integration.
Scanned document review and AI summary.
Voice dictation and conversation-to-clinical-note.
ICD, ICD-O and SNOMED CT support through a governed terminology service.
Clinician-approved medication/order templates.
TNM staging assistant using structured findings + biomarkers + versioned rules.
Treatment intent and clinician-approved care plan.
Visit summary and next appointment.
4.4 Laboratory
Physician order/external prescription intake.
Billing/payment status integration.
Patient/sample movement tracking.
Partner-lab handoff and status.
Electronic result integration where available.
PDF ingestion where necessary, with AI extraction + provenance + human verification.
Result completion status and reconciliation dashboard.
4.5 Radiology
Investigation order, scheduling and payment workflow.
Consent and patient arrival.
RIS/PACS/DICOM integration.
Study/report linkage to correct patient and encounter.
Consultant review and final report.
Any imaging AI must be separately validated and governed.
4.6 Conversion / Financial Counselling
Financial counselling record.
Drug and treatment estimates from governed pricing data.
Deterministic estimate calculations.
Fundraising-letter drafting from approved templates.
Assistance tracking.
LLM must never invent prices, doses, coverage or eligibility.
4.7 MDT / Tumor Board
Internal/virtual tumor-board scheduling.
External consultant access with case-level authorization.
Anonymous/minimum-necessary patient presentation.
Multi-user comments with author/timestamp.
Electronic approval and attendance.
Decision mapped to patient/encounter.
Follow-up task creation and tracking.
AI case brief, discussion capture and action extraction subject to governance.

5.1 Document AI
PDF/Image → classification → OCR/layout → clinical NLP → entities/relations→ terminology normalization → confidence/provenance → human verification → EMR
5.2 Conversation-to-clinical-note
Consented audio → ASR → speaker diarization → clinical NLP/LLM→ SOAP + oncology fields → source-linked draft → doctor edits/accepts → signed note
5.3 TNM staging
Pathology + imaging + clinical data + biomarkers→ AI extracts T/N/M inputs→ validation/completeness check→ deterministic versioned staging engine→ proposed stage + evidence→ oncologist verification→ official EMR status
Critical ruleDo not implement 'LLM determines cancer stage'. The LLM may extract/organize evidence; the final stage should come from a versioned staging engine/ruleset and be clinician-verified.
5.4 Clinical Decision Support
Hard safety rules should be deterministic.
Approved hospital protocols/guidelines must live in a versioned knowledge repository.
RAG should retrieve approved evidence; the LLM should not be treated as the knowledge source.
Display evidence and guideline version.
Require clinician action for high-risk outputs.
Log recommendation, clinician action and override reason.

8. Security, Privacy & Clinical Safety
Strong staff authentication and privileged-access controls.
RBAC plus case/purpose-level authorization where required.
External consultants see only assigned/approved cases.
Encryption in transit and at rest.
Central secrets/key management.
Separate development/test/production.
Do not use real patient data in development unless explicitly governed.
Audit sensitive record access and clinically significant changes.
Maintain backup/disaster recovery and manual fallback.
AI incident response must be able to disable unsafe models/features.
8.1 AI incident workflow
Incident → severity classification → protect patient → disable/contain if needed→ clinical review → root cause → corrective action → revalidation→ controlled release → close incident → update governance records

Regulatory note
This is a solution-architecture and governance blueprint, not legal advice or a regulatory determination. Whether a particular AI capability is regulated as medical-device software depends on its intended use, claims, functionality and applicable law. Diagnostic, treatment, prognostic and other high-risk clinical AI should undergo formal clinical, privacy, security and regulatory review before production.
20. Final Architecture Principle
The rule that should govern the entire programThe hospital's EMR and approved clinical sources remain the system of record. AI may extract, summarize, retrieve, flag, calculate or propose; high-risk clinical decisions remain under authorized human control. Data Governance makes the data trustworthy, protected and traceable. AI Governance makes the AI controlled, validated, monitored and accountable.
